## What the charge covers

Impaired driving allegations under the Canadian Criminal Code can involve several distinct offences, each with its own elements and its own defences. The Crown often proceeds on more than one and picks the strongest at trial.

- **Impaired operation** — driving while ability is affected by alcohol or drugs.
- **Over 80** — a blood alcohol concentration of 80&nbsp;mg or more per 100&nbsp;mL of blood within two hours of driving.
- **Refusal or failure to provide a sample** — declining a lawful demand.

<Callout label="In plain language">
&ldquo;Impaired&rdquo; is about how you drove. &ldquo;Over 80&rdquo; is about a number. &ldquo;Refusal&rdquo; is about what you said at the roadside. Different charges — different arguments.
</Callout>

## Why early legal advice matters

Timelines are short. Administrative licence suspensions run in parallel with the criminal file and can only be reviewed within a narrow window. Disclosure has to be requested properly. Statements made at the roadside and station affect what a Crown prosecutor is willing to consider months later.

## What we look at

- Charter compliance under sections 8, 9, and 10(b) — unlawful stops, delayed access to counsel, arbitrary detention.
- Breath-sample procedure — the &ldquo;as soon as practicable&rdquo; window, calibration records, qualified technician credentials.
- Grounds for the breath demand — reasonable grounds vs. hunch.
- Continuity of evidence — every hand-off from vehicle to instrument to disclosure package.
- Sentence mitigation where a conviction is likely.
