## What the Charge Actually Covers

Most people call it a "DUI," but the Criminal Code splits impaired-driving allegations into three distinct offences that often appear on the same information. Each has its own elements the Crown has to prove — and its own set of defences.

<IconCards>
  <IconCard icon="1" title="Impaired operation">
    Driving while your ability is affected by alcohol or drugs (s. 320.14(1)(a)). Argued on how you drove, not on a number.
  </IconCard>
  <IconCard icon="2" title="Over 80">
    A blood alcohol concentration of 80&nbsp;mg or more per 100&nbsp;mL within two hours of driving (s. 320.14(1)(b)). Turns on the reading and the procedure that produced it.
  </IconCard>
  <IconCard icon="3" title="Refusal">
    Declining a lawful demand to provide a breath sample (s. 320.15). Carries the same penalty range as a completed Over 80.
  </IconCard>
</IconCards>

<Callout label="In plain language">
&ldquo;Impaired&rdquo; is about how you drove. &ldquo;Over 80&rdquo; is about a number. &ldquo;Refusal&rdquo; is about what you said or didn&rsquo;t say at the roadside. Different charges — different arguments.
</Callout>

## How We Defend the Charge

Every file starts with a full disclosure review. We&rsquo;re looking, specifically, for the following issues — any one of which can change the outcome of the file:

- **Charter breaches** under sections 8, 9, and 10(b) — unlawful stops, arbitrary detention, delay in accessing counsel
- **Breath-sample technicalities** — the &ldquo;as soon as practicable&rdquo; window, calibration records, qualified-technician credentials, two-sample interval
- **Grounds for the breath demand** — did the officer actually have reasonable grounds, or was the demand made on a hunch
- **Continuity of evidence** — every hand-off from the vehicle to the instrument to the disclosure package
- **Sentence mitigation** where a conviction is likely — curative discharges, counselling records, Gladue considerations where they apply

When we identify a Charter issue, we bring the application. When we identify a technicality, we cross-examine on it at trial. When neither is available, we negotiate the resolution that leaves you in the best possible position — sometimes a lesser included offence, sometimes a Highway Traffic Act charge, sometimes a plea structured to preserve your ability to drive to work.

## Common Questions

### Do I have to plead guilty at the first appearance?

No, and you shouldn&rsquo;t. The first appearance is a scheduling event — it exists so the court can set the next date and confirm the Crown&rsquo;s election. Pleading guilty before disclosure has been reviewed is almost never in your interest.

### Can I keep driving while my case is going through court?

For most first-offence impaired charges, Ontario&rsquo;s Stream A ignition-interlock program lets qualifying offenders return to driving quickly if they plead guilty early. Whether it&rsquo;s the right move depends on the file — we&rsquo;ll walk through it with you.

### What if I refused the breath sample — is that better or worse?

Neither, in penalty terms. The Criminal Code treats refusal the same way it treats a full Over 80. But refusal cases turn on different evidence, and the officer&rsquo;s grounds for the demand become the central question at trial.
