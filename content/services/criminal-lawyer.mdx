## What Does a Criminal Charge Mean?

A criminal charge is a formal allegation that a person has committed an offence under Canadian criminal law. Being charged does not automatically mean you are guilty. The prosecution must prove the allegations according to the applicable legal standard, and you have rights throughout the criminal process.

The specific consequences of a charge depend on the offence, the circumstances surrounding the allegation, your history, and many other factors.

## Penalties for a Criminal Offence

The potential consequences of a criminal conviction vary significantly depending on the offence and circumstances of the case. Some offences can result in substantial fines, probation, restrictions, a criminal record, or imprisonment.

A conviction can also create consequences outside the courtroom. Depending on your circumstances, a criminal record may affect employment opportunities, professional licensing, travel, housing, family matters, and other areas of your life.

### The Consequences Depend on the Charge

There is no single penalty that applies to every criminal charge. Courts consider the nature and seriousness of the offence and other relevant circumstances when determining an appropriate outcome.

For example, an impaired driving matter may involve different legal considerations from an assault charge, while a serious criminal offence may carry substantially different consequences.

### A Criminal Record Can Have Long-Term Effects

Many people are particularly concerned about avoiding a criminal record. That concern is understandable. However, the best approach is not to assume what the outcome will be before the facts and legal issues have been properly reviewed.

Our role is to assess the circumstances, identify potential weaknesses in the prosecution's case, protect your legal rights, and pursue the best possible result available in your circumstances.
